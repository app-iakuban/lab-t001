Why Spain

Where the solid trucks are, and why you need someone there

  • Dry climate, little salt

    Much of Spain is dry and its roads see little salt, so many old Defenders there still have solid chassis, outriggers and floors. Plenty of UK and northern European trucks don't.

  • Listed in Spanish, sold locally

    The good ones sit on Spanish sites, described in Spanish, by sellers who rarely answer an email from abroad and have never shipped a truck to America.

  • Photos hide rot

    Listing photos rarely show the chassis. Someone has to get under the truck and check the papers before you send money.

US shops that build Defenders source donor trucks from Europe too, and some call southern Spain their favorite place to buy: dry climate, limited road salt, left-hand drive.From the sourcing page of a Connecticut Defender builder, 2025

Questions

Before you ask

Can I legally import a Defender from Spain into the US?

Yes, if it was built at least 25 years ago, counted to the month of manufacture shown on the VIN plate. The first registration date doesn't count, and a truck first registered in mid-2001 may have been built months earlier. As of October 2026 the cutoff is October 2001, and it moves forward one month every month. Trucks this old are exempt from US safety standards (DOT form HS-7). EPA exempts vehicles 21 years or older in original configuration, so an engine swap can cause trouble. We read the build date off the plate and match it to the Spanish papers before we recommend a truck.

How much is the import duty?

As of October 2026, 10% of the purchase price for passenger vehicles imported from the EU (tariff heading 8703), which covers Defender Station Wagons. Pickups and some hard tops are classed as trucks (heading 8704) and pay 25%. US Customs decides from the VIN and body type, so when it's unclear we show you both numbers. On top of duty come small customs processing and harbor fees, usually around $100 to $150. Rates change, which is why each estimate is dated.

I live in California. Can I register one?

Possibly not. California has its own emissions rules, and imported diesels like the Td5 and 300Tdi can be very hard to register there. We'd rather tell you that before you spend anything, so if you're in California, mention it in the form and we'll talk it through first. Other states have their own inspection rules too, so it's worth a call to your DMV.

Is it cheaper than buying in the US?

Compared with what US dealers ask for similar 110 wagons, usually yes, by several thousand dollars. Compared with a lucky auction result, it can come out about even. What you get is a truck from a dry region, checked in person, with every cost to your port written down before you commit.

Which models do you look for?

Land Rover Defender 90 and 110 Station Wagon, with the 200Tdi, 300Tdi or Td5, built in October 2001 or earlier. A Mercedes-Benz G-Class 300GD is possible on request. Pickups and hard tops are possible too, but they pay 25% duty.

How long does it take?

The shortlist takes 72 hours. The inspection is usually within one to two weeks of your pick. Shipping from Spain to the US East Coast is usually two to four weeks at sea, plus time in port and customs. Plan on roughly six to ten weeks from your decision to pickup. We can't promise a customs release date.

You pay the seller directly, or through an escrow service such as Escrow.com that releases the money once the agreed conditions are met. We never hold money for the truck. Our $3,000 fee is invoiced separately and only after you decide to go ahead with a specific truck.

Who inspects the truck?

We inspect it in person before you commit. You get photos and video of the chassis, outriggers, floors, bulkhead, engine, papers and VIN plate, and we tell you the bad news too. An inspection is a careful look, not a warranty: it can miss hidden problems.

Can you guarantee a specific truck?

No. Until the truck is inspected and bought, the seller can sell it to someone else. If that happens, we find you another one.

Are you a dealer?
